Bike Buyers Sales Analysis & Dashboard (Excel)
Project Overview

This project analyzes a customer dataset from a bike retailer to understand which customer characteristics (income, gender, commute distance, age) are associated with purchasing a bike. The workflow covers data cleaning, pivot table analysis, and an interactive Excel dashboard with slicers for filtering by Marital Status, Education, and Region.

Tools
Microsoft Excel
Pivot Tables
Excel Charts
Slicers (interactive filters)
Workflow
Raw Data (bike_buyers sheet) — original dataset of 1,000 customer records with fields including Marital Status, Gender, Income, Children, Education, Occupation, Home Owner, Cars, Commute Distance, Region, Age, and Purchased Bike.
Data Cleaning (Working Sheet)
Expanded abbreviated values into full labels (e.g., "M" → "Married", "F" → "Female")
Added a calculated Age Brackets column (Adolescent / Middle Age / Old) to group customers for easier analysis
Pivot Tables (Pivot Table sheet)
Average Income by Gender, split by Purchase status
Count of Purchases by Commute Distance
Count of Purchases by Age Bracket
Interactive Dashboard (Dashboard sheet)
Charts: Average Income per Purchase (by Gender), Customer Commute Distance, Customer Age Brackets
Slicers: Marital Status, Education, Region — allowing dynamic filtering of all charts at once
Key Insights
Overall bike purchase rate: 48.1% (481 of 1,000 customers)
Customers with a short commute (0–1 miles) were the most likely to purchase (200 Yes vs. 166 No)
Customers with a long commute (10+ miles) were far less likely to purchase (33 Yes vs. 78 No) — commute distance appears to be a strong signal
Middle-aged customers made up the largest share of both buyers and non-buyers, and had the highest raw count of purchases (388)
Average income among buyers was slightly higher for males (~$60,124) than females (~$55,774)
